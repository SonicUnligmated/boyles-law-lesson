# Boyle's law

An interactive lesson on Boyle's law for a fixed mass of air at constant temperature, and on finding the atmospheric pressure from the graph of pressure against the reciprocal of volume.

Six sections:

1. Practical: a glass tube of trapped air above oil with a printed cm³ scale, an oil reservoir with a Bourdon gauge, a valve and a hand pump. Pump strokes raise the pressure; opening the valve slightly lets it fall. Compression warms the air a little and it settles back to room temperature over about a minute, shown by an optional thermometer, so readings taken too soon are flagged. A magnifier helps read the meniscus and the needle. The table computes 1/V and (P + P₀)V, the graph of P against 1/V has a draggable line extended to the P-axis, and the calculation gives the atmospheric pressure and its percentage error. A guided tutorial runs through every step.
2. Aim and outcomes.
3. Theory: pressure from molecular impacts, why each reading waits for room temperature, gauge pressure and the intercept −P₀, breathing, ventilators and the spirometer.
4. Method.
5. Observation and calculation, with worked example values.
6. Multiple-choice questions.

Formula: P ∝ 1/V at constant temperature. With a gauge reading P, (P + P₀)V = k, so P = k(1/V) − P₀.

In the calculation panel, every symbol and number can be hovered or tapped to show its meaning and where it comes from.

The site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step. Open `index.html` or serve the folder with any static file server.

Fonts: Oxanium, Exo 2, Noto Sans and Noto Sans Math (SIL Open Font License).
