Lab 10 B

Boyle's law

Aim

To verify Boyle's law and determine the atmospheric pressure.

Intended learning outcomes

  • Explain the relationship between the volume and pressure of a gas at constant temperature.
  • Determine the relation between pressure and volume of the gas.

Medical perspectives

The breathing mechanism

The diaphragm and rib muscles enlarge the chest, the lung volume grows and the pressure in the alveoli falls a little below atmospheric, so air flows in. Relaxing them shrinks the volume, raises the pressure and air flows out.

Ventilators and anaesthesia machines

A ventilator delivers each breath by raising the pressure at the airway; the volume delivered follows from the pressure and the stiffness of the lungs. Anaesthesia machines and oxygen cylinders use P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ to work out how much gas a compressed cylinder can supply.

Respiratory physiology

Spirometers measure the volumes breathed in and out, and body plethysmographs find the gas volume of the lungs from small pressure changes, both relying on how pressure and volume trade off at constant temperature.

Lab 10 B · Theory

Pressure, volume and temperature

Pressure from molecular impacts

A gas is a crowd of molecules moving fast in random directions. Each time one strikes a wall it reverses part of its momentum, and the steady rain of impacts is the pressure. Squeeze the same molecules into half the volume and each part of the wall is hit twice as often, at the same speed if the temperature has not changed, so the pressure doubles.

  1. Kinetic theory gives the pressure of N molecules of mass m with mean square speed ⟨c²⟩.P V = ⅓ N m ⟨c²⟩
  2. The mean square speed depends only on the temperature.½ m ⟨c²⟩ = 3⁄2 kB T
  3. So for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature the product is fixed.P V = constant, P ∝ 1/V

Fixed mass of air at 25 °C with PV = 48.7 atm cm³ (absolute pressure). The dots on the small graphs show the present state.

Constant temperature: why each reading waits

Pushing the oil up does work on the air, and for a moment the air is a few degrees warmer than the room; letting it expand cools it. A warm gas pushes harder, so a reading taken at once gives a V (and a P) slightly too large and the product PV comes out high.

The glass and the oil carry the heat away, and within about a minute the air is back at room temperature. Only then is the process isothermal, the condition Boyle's law needs.

Gauge pressure and the atmosphere

A Bourdon gauge compares the pressure inside with the air outside: it reads 0 when the valve is open to the room. Its reading P is the gauge pressure, the excess over the atmospheric pressure P0. The trapped air feels the absolute pressure P + P0.

  1. Boyle's law for the absolute pressure.(P + P₀) V = k
  2. Divide by V and rearrange.P = k (1/V) − P₀

So P against 1/V is a straight line of slope k that meets the P-axis at −P0. The size of the intercept is the atmospheric pressure, about 1 atm (101.3 kPa).

Breathing, ventilators and the spirometer

In quiet breathing the chest enlarges the lungs by about half a litre; the alveolar pressure dips by roughly 1 mmHg below atmospheric, enough for air to flow in. A ventilator reverses the drive: it raises the airway pressure to push the breath in.

A spirometer records these volumes. In the classic design the breath lifts or lowers a light bell floating over water; a counterweight and pen trace the volume against time. Modern units measure the flow electronically and integrate it.

A cylinder of oxygen holding 10 L at about 137 atm gauge (138 atm absolute) gives about 138 × 10 ÷ 1 ≈ 1380 L at atmospheric pressure: Boyle's law on the absolute pressures, at constant temperature.

Lab 10 B · Method

Procedure

Apparatus

ApparatusName and role
Glass tube with scale
Holds the fixed mass of air above the oil; the printed scale gives its volume in cm³ at the meniscus.
Oil reservoir
A steel cylinder of oil; air pumped in above the oil pushes it up the tube.
Bourdon gauge
A curved tube that straightens under pressure and turns the needle; reads the gauge pressure in atm.
Valve
Holds the pressure when closed; opened slightly it lets air out slowly.
Air pump
A hand pump with a non-return valve; each stroke pushes air into the reservoir.
Thermometer (optional)
Shows the air warming on compression and cooling back to room temperature.

Steps

Good practice
  • Read the scale with the eye level with the meniscus, and read the needle from straight in front of the dial.
  • Wait about a minute after every change before reading: the air must be back at room temperature.
  • Open the valve only slightly, so the pressure falls slowly and can be stopped near the value wanted.
  • Take the readings from the highest pressure downwards and spread them over the whole scale.
Care
  • Do not pump beyond the maximum of the gauge; the tube and joints are made for that pressure only.
  • Release the pressure slowly at the end, so the oil does not surge up the tube.

Common slips

P against V

P against V is a curve. Boyle's law gives a straight line only for P against 1/V.

Sign of the intercept

The line meets the P-axis below zero, at c = −P₀. The atmospheric pressure is −c, a positive number.

Reading too soon

Straight after a change the air is warmer or cooler than the room and PV is off by about 1 %. Wait a minute.

Gauge pressure in PV

The gauge reads the excess pressure. PV is constant only with the absolute pressure P + P₀.
